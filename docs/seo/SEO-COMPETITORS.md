**Research phase:** Phases 1–2 — public search-landscape discovery and competitor verification  
**Research date:** 2026-08-27  
**Status:** Research in progress; not an implementation plan  
**Client:** Jones Pressure Washing  
**Website:** [jonespressurewashingnj.com](https://www.jonespressurewashingnj.com/)  

## Scope used for this phase

The detailed SEO scope questionnaire is still being completed. This phase uses the business direction already established in the meeting notes:

- Priority services: soft washing and holiday lighting
- Soft-washing focus: residential house washing and roof washing
- Priority soft-washing markets: Nutley, Livingston, and Verona, New Jersey
- Holiday-lighting market: Essex County plus Bergen County from Paramus southward
- Commercial soft-washing and pressure-washing options are not part of the intended marketing focus

Primary working objective: earn qualified residential quote requests for services Jones Pressure Washing can accurately and profitably deliver. Rankings and traffic are supporting indicators, not the final outcome.

## Method and limitations

This phase used public web-search snapshots and direct inspection of representative public competitor pages.

- Research date: 2026-08-27
- Searcher location: not pinned to a physical address in Nutley, Livingston, Verona, Essex County, or Paramus
- Device: search-provider default; not controlled
- Personalization: unknown
- Search engine result type: public web results supplied by the research tool
- Local pack and map results: not reliably exposed by this tool and therefore not evaluated in this phase
- Paid placements: not reliably exposed and therefore not evaluated
- Result order: not reported as a ranking claim
- Review counts, guarantees, credentials, pricing, and other competitor claims are recorded only as statements observed on competitor-controlled pages unless independently verified
- Search results and competitor pages can change; observations are dated rather than treated as permanent facts

This document does not infer why a page appears in search results and does not treat a visible competitor tactic as a ranking factor.

## Representative page comparisons

### Soft washing

#### Gallo Bros Power Washing

Observed facts:

- The Verona page separates soft washing from roof cleaning, house washing, power washing, solar-panel cleaning, and other adjacent services.
- The page presents a direct quote CTA, local phone number, review count, insurance/background-check claims, and a scheduling flow.
- It explains the difference between low-pressure soft washing and high-pressure cleaning.

Interpretation:

- The strongest feature is not the number of services or pages. It is the quick connection between the user's surface/problem, a safer method, proof, and a clear next step.
- Review counts, same-week scheduling, and safety claims remain competitor-controlled claims unless independently verified.

Do not copy:

- Broad city-page expansion, wording, FAQs, review presentation, or unsupported guarantees.

#### TruClean Wash

Observed facts:

- Its Livingston result documents one identifiable roof-and-house cleaning project.
- The page describes the precipitating problem, roof conditions, method, location, customer decision to add house washing, and a customer review.
- The page connects the project to relevant services and a quote path.

Interpretation:

- This is the strongest sampled soft-washing example of first-party project proof. It gives the page a reason to exist beyond changing the town name.
- JPW can compete authentically only with real projects, real images, and client-approved details of its own.

#### Rinse Prince

Observed facts:

- The Livingston house-washing page describes contaminants, surfaces, process steps, plant protection, optional before-and-after documentation, FAQs, and related services.
- It mentions oxidation removal for aluminum siding.
- It provides a direct quote CTA and contact information.

Interpretation:

- Clear surface/process information can reduce uncertainty for homeowners and can support the proposed oxidation-removal research.
- The page is long and contains numerous precise claims. Length itself is not a model to imitate, and every JPW process claim must come from actual operations.

#### Softwash Ranger

Observed facts:

- The Nutley page presents residential, commercial, estate, realtor, and specialty soft-washing offers.
- It publishes roof-cleaning warranty language, customer testimonials, insurance/licensing language, and an instant-quote CTA.
- Its service area covers many counties and multiple states.

Interpretation:

- The range of offers and warranty language can help users understand the provider, but it also creates a much broader position than JPW's planned house-and-roof focus.
- JPW should not retain commercial messaging merely because a competitor does; the client direction is residential focus.

#### Under Pressure House Washing

Observed facts:

- The sampled Verona page is narrowly focused on roof washing, black streaks, moss, and a low-pressure method.
- It presents one primary quote action and local contact information.

Interpretation:

- Narrow task alignment can be clearer than a page attempting to sell every exterior service.
- Local architectural commentary should be treated cautiously unless backed by genuine operating experience.

#### NJ Property Service

Observed facts:

- The company publishes a Nutley address and local phone number.
- Its site is simpler than the specialist sites and focuses on residential and commercial pressure washing, before-and-after presentation, and direct contact.

Interpretation:

- This is geographically closer to the target customer than many broader North Jersey providers.
- A visually or technically simpler website can still be a meaningful business competitor. Competitive research should not equate site polish with market strength.

## Unmet needs and authentic differentiation to investigate

These are research directions, not approved page changes:

1. **Real project evidence in the three soft-washing priority towns.** The sampled set contains many generic location pages but fewer pages grounded in identifiable work.
2. **Clear house-versus-roof decision support.** Users should understand which method applies to which surface without exaggerated damage or longevity claims.
3. **Honest surface and service limitations.** Explaining what JPW will not wash, when oxidation needs separate treatment, or when a roof should not be cleaned may create credible differentiation.
4. **A fully explained holiday-lighting relationship.** Ownership, quoting, installation, maintenance, removal, storage, and future seasons should be accurate and internally consistent.
5. **Low-friction qualification.** Address/photo intake may be valuable if it matches JPW's quoting process and the existing CRM can support it.
6. **Transferable exterior-services trust.** Real reviews about communication, property care, safety, and reliability may support holiday lighting without pretending those reviews concern lights.
7. **First-party photographs with useful context.** Before/after images are more useful when the page explains the property condition, service selected, method, and result.

## Competitor tactics and assumptions to avoid

- Copying competitor copy, FAQs, packages, guarantees, pricing, location lists, schema, or page templates
- Treating town-page volume as proof that JPW needs more town pages
- Publishing generic architectural or neighborhood descriptions without real local experience
- Preserving commercial-service messaging after the client has chosen a residential focus
- Treating review count, page length, keyword repetition, or number of FAQs as ranking factors
- Repeating claims that soft washing always lasts a fixed number of times longer
- Publishing environmental, plant-safety, licensing, insurance, warranty, response-time, or damage-prevention claims without evidence
- Using a competitor's minimum price as JPW's pricing recommendation
- Treating one unlocalized search snapshot as a stable ranking report
- Assuming an organic-result competitor also competes in the local pack

## Phase 2 continuation — expanded and verified competitor research

### Phase 2 method

Phase 2 continued from the Phase 1 result set. It did not restart competitor discovery or treat the larger sample as a rank report.

- Research date: 2026-08-27
- Organic-result source: public web-search snapshots
- Organic-search geography: geography included in the query, but the searcher's physical location was not pinned to the target town
- Organic-search device: search-provider default; not controlled
- Organic-search personalization: unknown
- Map-result source: unsigned Google Maps in the Codex in-app desktop browser
- Map-result geography: query text included the target place; browser location permission was not used and the searcher's physical location was not pinned
- Map-result device: desktop browser; viewport not overridden
- Map-result personalization: unsigned, but other personalization and IP-based effects remain unknown
- Paid-result handling: clearly labeled sponsored map results were recorded separately and not treated as local-pack competitors
- Result order: a dated snapshot only; no stable ranking claim is made
- Direct inspection: representative public pages were opened where the research tool could retrieve them; inaccessible pages remain search-snippet evidence only

Material, cautious takeaways:

- `roof cleaning` and `house washing` produced more measurable relative interest in this New Jersey comparison than `soft washing`; this supports researching customer-problem language rather than relying only on industry terminology.
- `christmas light installation` produced slightly more measurable relative interest than `holiday light installation`, but both were sparse at the state level. The comparison is insufficient to choose page wording or forecast demand.

- The lighting terms showed intermittent late-summer through holiday-season activity, but low-volume zeros and sampling prevent a precise launch-window conclusion from this single comparison.

No content-plan decision is made from this Trends sample. Search Console, Keyword Planner, CRM, and repeated seasonal comparisons would materially improve confidence.
